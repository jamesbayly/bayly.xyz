I spent two days in Armenia, heading out of the city on Russian-speaking tours each morning. This is not enough time to understand a country. It is, apparently, enough time to become quite unreasonable about its architectural heritage.

![Tatev, the best of them all](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-WvkfXGw/1/LgXhM93Qkx5rxqcfJM6TmzwcMJdnQsJXDtdvJfVsr/X3/DSC03726-X3.jpg)

A medieval monastery in a spectacular mountain setting? Lovely.

![](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-XfPVMbn/0/LDSNbdr6gPsxRb2PKMdXK69GCdtvRmXF6ZsQFxSSs/X3/DSC03672-Pano-X3.jpg)

Another one? Incredible.

![](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-rSQ2wK6/0/NDdQvbdgXtfxCzdc2CQX45b4fh2jkq5nQvdmFzLWK/X3/DSC03740-X3.jpg)

Another one?

Armenia had spent centuries producing these places, I had given myself forty-eight hours to appreciate them. Somewhere along the way, a thousand years of history and an exceptionally well-chosen cliff stopped being enough.

![Noravank against the red cliffs](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-5nSZhfv/1/NFr4jFB2NsgmTSTnn3cJzGmnKWHDVVzz4nr2K9KRj/X3/DSC03708-X3.jpg)

Noravank sits against towering red cliffs, the sort of setting that would make a reasonably ordinary building look magnificent. The medieval Armenians had put a magnificent building there instead.

![Noravank in its surroundings](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-HrFJ9QK/1/LRj4Kg9Zkwhjk3x253z2VxZ5mp2QkqK8gFstbbVbG/X2/DSC03707-X2.jpg)

At Sevanavank, the backdrop changes to Lake Sevan. Different landscape, more ancient churches. Even an actual wedding was taking place in this one.

![Wedding at Sevanavank](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-RDfVwXf/1/NdNMLkkWjwDD3gtR9D783HJhTst4KZzZBDxDTxxqh/X3/DSC03687-X3.jpg)

![Sevanavank and Lake Sevan](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-rrdr4tj/1/NFLWmXD47vTNHG9zk6VmPTP9nT3r7nRrHkt8kDgPN/X3/DSC03690-X3.jpg)

Geghard offers a further variation by being partly carved into the mountain itself. Most builders encounter a cliff and adjust the plans. Here, somebody had decided the cliff was the building material.

![Tombs at Geghard](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-6Xw2ZxG/1/LcbZhWZLxT3LTcpps6HTqprxqRZBBMMCBNGSnHJvf/X3/DSC03662-X3.jpg)

Garni, at least, broke the pattern. Roman columns. A bathhouse with a mosaic floor. Something that looks as though it belongs on the cover of a textbook about ancient Greece. A pagan imposter.

What is all this doing in the Caucasus?

![Garni temple above the gorge.](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-pm6twZN/1/KtbSFsdbH4bnzfbFNZKDmvm2Pf698Kg663VNjxrpb/X3/DSC03682-X3.jpg)

For centuries, Rome and Parthia competed for influence over Armenia, including who got to occupy its throne. In AD 66 a deal was made, the Parthian prince Tiridates travelled to Rome to be crowned king of Armenia by Nero. One empire supplied the candidate; the other supplied the ceremony. Both could claim a win.

![Roman columns at Garni](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-XMf4Pss/1/Lqf7pLxjHPpnhn9PZtPzhQXLJpdvTTn9GTDhfgc4w/X3/DSC03677-X3.jpg)

Armenia wasn't simply a blank space between empires. It had its own rulers and ambitions. Unfortunately, its neighbours also had ambitions for it. How do you keep a country, or a culture, intact when larger neighbouring powers keep rearranging the region?

![Inside the church](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-3cVJTWT/0/Nh2DHqGc7M5C4LfFCC9QncN5xbdCmZ4gC9qbGjLDB/X3/DSC03730-X3.jpg)

It's the reason for the monasteries - Armenia adopted Christianity in the early fourth century. Over the centuries, its monasteries became much more than places to pray. Geghard had a school, a library, and a scriptorium. But nearly all included defensive walls and princely tombs. Education, religion, protection, and the interests of powerful families all occupied the same site.

![A musician earns his living](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-t867kGL/0/Kg6wxbdW4Lv3z58dtdtht4nZ4g7W4D8VvjXb3r2wh/X2/DSC03652-X2.jpg)

The modern map echoes the past. For ordinary overland travel, half of Armenia's borders, with Turkey and Azerbaijan, remain closed, leaving Georgia to the north and Iran to the south as Armenia's only land connections to the outside world. Difficult neighbours are hardly confined to the history books.

![Christianity Remains](https://photos.smugmug.com/Armenia/i-MX4xxgj/1/LvMt49StWJjkDTnxcp6Z7NLtcnRVcxqNt6CTccQVz/X3/DSC03698-X3.jpg)
