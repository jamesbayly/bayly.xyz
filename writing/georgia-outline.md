# Georgia essay outline: The View North

Status: working outline, not a draft for publication.

## Core idea

Georgia is close enough to Russia that the relationship appears in the tourist economy, language, road, and historical memory. A country can welcome visitors, earn money from them, and still be deeply wary of the state they came from. The piece should ask how Georgians live with that proximity, not pretend a short visit can answer for them.

The narrator is implicated. James found Russian-language tour buses much cheaper and used Google Translate and AI tools to make them work. In Georgia he bought a tour from burly-looking men advertising on the street with Cyrillic signboards, then travelled towards Gergeti among Russian tourists. That is a first-hand way into the story, stronger than opening with abstract geopolitics. He also found Tbilisi cosmopolitan, full of high-end shops, and simply a lovely city. The ordinary pleasures of Georgia should challenge the expectation of a place consumed by fear. His comparative stops from the preceding months are Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and Azerbaijan.

## Narrative beats, approximately 650 to 800 words

1. **The cheaper seat.** Open with the Cyrillic signboards, burly-looking tour sellers, and the Russian-language bus towards Gergeti. Do not invent dialogue. Briefly recall finding similar Russian-facing tourism options in Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and Azerbaijan, as James's observation rather than a claim about each country's entire industry.
2. **A city that refuses the expected mood.** Tbilisi felt cosmopolitan and prosperous on its main street. Use a concrete sight from James's visit. Sighnaghi can provide a second, short glimpse of Georgia on its own terms. Neither city should be reduced to a symbol of Russia.
3. **The awkward monument.** On the road north, stop at the Russia–Georgia Friendship Monument. South Ossetia lies just west of the route towards Gergeti. Its proximity and the monument's name put the question on the page. Give one observed detail of the monument, view, or crowd, then one compact historical turn: the 2008 war centred on South Ossetia and also involved Abkhazia. James sees it as an early warning of what later happened in Ukraine, but the two wars are not identical and the 2008 sequence must not be simplified.
4. **Gergeti as the visual centre.** Follow the road to Stepantsminda and the Gergeti Trinity Church beneath Mkinvartsveri, also called Mount Kazbegi. Reserve at least two, ideally three, of the best photos for this final third: wide landscape, church and mountain, then a human-scale or close detail if James photographed one. Describe only actual weather, light, visitors, and experiences. Let the physical view north carry the theme without making the mountain itself a political allegory.
5. **An ending that stays honest.** Return to the Russian-language bus or the word "friendship", then to a specific Gergeti image. Accommodation is not the same as trust; a visitor cannot diagnose an entire country's feelings. End on the scene, not a verdict or travel slogan.

No body subheadings are needed in the finished essay. Possible alternate title: *The Friendship Tour*.

## Factual spine and limits

- Georgia's tourism agency recorded 1,579,764 **visitor trips** from Russia in 2025. These are trips, not unique people, and not all are tourists: [2025 tourism overview](https://api.gnta.ge/storage/files/doc/statistical-data-overview-2025.pdf).
- A CRRC/NDI survey after the 2022 Russian influx found 69% expected large Russian arrivals to have a negative impact. That measures attitudes at the time to the influx, not every Russian tourist or current public opinion: [CRRC analysis](https://crrc.ge/how-do-georgians-feel-about-the-influx-of-russians/).
- The independent EU-commissioned inquiry found Georgia's shelling of Tskhinvali began large-scale hostilities after a long escalation; it also found much of Russia's subsequent campaign far exceeded reasonable defence. Keep the 2008-to-Ukraine connection clearly as James's interpretation: [fact-finding report](https://www.mpil.de/files/pdf4/IIFFMCG_Volume_I1.pdf).
- Mkinvartsveri is the peak, also called Mount Kazbegi; Gergeti refers to the church and locality below it: [Georgia Travel](https://georgia.travel/9-amazing-sights-in-mtskheta-mtianeti).
- "Russian travellers have few other options" is an inference to research before making it a factual claim. The strong first-hand claim is that James found Russian-language tours cheaper and more prevalent in the places he visited.
