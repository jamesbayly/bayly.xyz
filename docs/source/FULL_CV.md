**Overarching philosophy:** "Augmented Public Good" — AI positioned as institutional capability shift, not an IT project. North Star is mission delivery, service quality, and enablement.

**Three positioning frames** for clients to select based on mandate and political context:

- Frame A: The Responsive Organisation — faster, 24/7, accessible organisation
- Frame B: The Anticipatory Organisation — predictive intervention before needs escalate
- Frame C: The Trustworthy Organisation — transparency, auditability, procedural fairness

**SPEAR Rubric (opportunity identification filter):**
Five-dimension evaluation filter for organisational AI opportunities, deliberately weighted toward process automation:

- Scale & Suitability — high-volume, low-discretion, well-documented processes. Red flag: Māori data sovereignty obligations assessed at intake, not retrofitted
- Problem Fit — is the nature of the problem suited to AI? Red flag: human judgment IS the value
- Explainability — can the process be explained in plain language to a citizen, minister, or OIA request?
- Acceptability — will staff trust it? Will citizens accept it? Union/PSA engagement precondition
- Return (Public Value) + Counterfactual — what public good does this produce AND is AI genuinely the best intervention? Requires documenting non-AI alternatives considered (doing nothing, process redesign, conventional automation, adding staff)

**Six-Pillar Governance & Operating Model:**

1. Governance & Accountability
2. Security, Data Sovereignty & Technical Readiness
3. Monitoring, Observability & Incident Response
4. Training & Workforce Evolution
5. Ethics & Equity
6. Legal & Regulatory Alignment

**Procurement & Model Selection Framework:**
Four-category make-vs-buy matrix: Buy (commodity AI), Adapt (RAG on organisation content), Build (mission-unique AI on private infrastructure), Borrow (inter-organisation shared services).
